Selected work

Projects

Four projects that between them cover most of what I do: an interpretable agent for single-cell genomics, two studies asking whether self-pretraining really earns its keep, and a comparative study of deep models for human locomotion. Each entry links to the paper and, where available, the preprint.

Full publication list · Google Scholar · GitHub

  1. 01

    ELISA — an interpretable agent for single-cell genomics

    Briefings in Bioinformatics (Oxford University Press) · accepted 2026 · sole author

    ELISA is a hybrid generative AI agent for expression-grounded discovery in single-cell data. The design point is interpretability: every claim the agent makes has to be tied back to measured expression, so an annotation can be traced to the cells and genes that support it rather than to the language model's priors. That constraint is what makes the output usable as evidence instead of as a plausible-sounding summary.

  2. 02

    Towards understanding self-pretraining for sequence classification

    Under review · earlier version at the ES-FoMo III workshop on efficient systems for foundation models, ICML 2025 · with L. Zollo, P. Soda and A. Orvieto

    Self-pretraining — pretraining a transformer on the very dataset it will later be fine-tuned on — is widely reported to help sequence classification. This project asks how much of that gain survives a properly tuned randomly initialised baseline, which turns the interesting question from whether the trick works into which data and optimisation regimes it actually needs. Carried out in the Deep Models and Optimization group at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems and the ELLIS Institute Tübingen.

  3. 03

    Deep learning for human locomotion analysis in lower-limb exoskeletons

    Frontiers in Computer Science, vol. 7, 2025 · with C. Tamantini, M. Tortora and colleagues

    A comparative study of deep learning architectures for analysing human locomotion from data recorded on instrumented lower-limb exoskeletons. The signals are the awkward kind — multivariate, noisy, and scarce relative to the models people want to apply to them — so the value of the study is in putting the architectures under one shared protocol and seeing which differences hold up. Work done in the Unit of Advanced Robotics and Human-Centered Technologies at Università Campus Bio-Medico di Roma.

  4. 04

    Is self-pretraining really useful to improve diagnosis in medical time series?

    Submitted to the NeurIPS workshop on Responsible Communication of Machine Learning Research in Biomedicine · with A. Orvieto, P. Soda and L. Zollo

    The clinical counterpart to the sequence-classification study. If self-pretraining helps anywhere it ought to help here, because labelled clinical recordings are exactly what you never have enough of. Testing that against careful baselines gives a deflationary answer, which is why the work is framed as much around how such results get communicated in biomedicine as around the result itself. Presented as a talk at the MedicinAI workshop, University of Trento, in December 2025.
