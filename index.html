Profile

About

I completed the Italian National PhD Programme in Artificial Intelligence (Health and Life Sciences track) at Università Campus Bio-Medico di Roma, with a thesis on Artificial Intelligence Applications to Lower-Limb Rehabilitation Robotics, discussed in July 2026.

From October 2024 to September 2026 I worked in the Deep Models and Optimization group at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems and the ELLIS Institute Tübingen, with Prof. Antonio Orvieto, on when — and whether — self-pretraining actually helps transformers on sequence classification. A recurring theme in my work is checking that reported gains survive careful baselines: several of my papers report deflationary results, because I think those are the ones the field is missing.

My background is unusual for an AI researcher. I started in biotechnology and moved through quantitative and computational biology before machine learning. That path is why I care about methods that hold up on real biomedical data — noisy, irregular, and rarely abundant enough for the models we like to use.

Interests

Research directions

01

Self-supervised pretraining

Understanding when self-pretraining genuinely improves sequence classification, and when it merely matches a well-tuned random initialisation. Rigorous baselines, honest ablations.

02

Neural models for time series

Architectures for irregular, asynchronous and multivariate signals — the kind produced by clinical monitoring, wearable sensors and instrumented exoskeletons.

03

Agentic AI for biology

Interpretable hybrid agents that ground their reasoning in measured expression, so single-cell analysis stays traceable back to the data rather than to the language model.

04

AI for rehabilitation robotics

Deep learning for human locomotion analysis and intent detection in lower-limb exoskeletons, from literature synthesis to comparative benchmarking.
